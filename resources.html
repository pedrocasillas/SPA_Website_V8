Two simple ways to check the fundamentals and see how I approach a pitch.

Quick clarity check

Six questions every investment pitch must answer

01

Who are you?

The founders, company and relevant ability to execute.

02

What do you do?

The customer problem, solution and value proposition.

03

What progress have you made?

Customers, revenue, pilots, users or other evidence.

04

How does the business make money?

The commercial model and assumptions that matter.

05

What are you raising?

The amount, use of funds, timeframe and milestone.

06

How could an investor receive a return?

The growth logic, potential outcomes and timing.

Worked example

From vague claim to practical priority

Fictional example: this demonstrates the method and is not presented as a client result.
Founder statement
“We help independent clinics reduce missed appointments with an AI booking assistant. We are raising £500,000 to accelerate growth.”
What is clear

The customer, broad problem, type of solution and amount being raised.

What an investor may still need

Evidence of adoption, the commercial model, use of capital and the measurable milestone the investment should achieve.

Priority

Connect the £500,000 to a specific commercial plan and show which assumptions have already been tested.

Could somebody outside the company explain your case back to you?

What they omit or misunderstand is useful evidence.
